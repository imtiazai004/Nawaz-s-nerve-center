# The week — from 2026-08-02

Written up front, at the owner's request, before starting. **Order is deliberate**, not a
preference: each block removes something the next one would otherwise have to work around.

**Nothing here waits on the district.** Everything they owe is in
[`for-the-district.md`](for-the-district.md), and every item below has a placeholder standing
in for whatever has not arrived. If a block turns out to need an answer I do not have, I put a
visible stand-in behind it, add a row to that file, and carry on.

**The one thing I cannot do myself is the M1 gate**, which says *a real Rescue operator, no
developer present*. I can build everything up to it and prove it works; I cannot prove it is
usable by someone who did not build it. That is **R-12**.

## Block 2 — M1: Rescue 1122, in full · three days

The milestone. One department taken all the way down, because the real shape of a department
workspace can only be found by building one properly. Four built shallowly in parallel produce
four mini-apps and a wrong abstraction that then has to be unwound.

| # | Task | Acceptance |
|---|---|---|
| M1-01 | **The department workspace** — one screen an operator lives in for a shift | Live incidents for my department, my inbox, my roster, and what needs me now. Not four tabs of the same list filtered differently |
| M1-02 | **Resources and teams** — vehicles, crews, equipment, as data | A resource belongs to a department, has a status, and is editable from the roster screen. No hardcoded fleet |
| M1-03 | **Dispatch** — assign resources and a team to an incident | `assigned` already exists as an event and nothing produces it from a screen. Dispatch is the act that turns an acknowledgement into a response |
| M1-04 | **Response action logging** — what was done, when, by whom | Append-only, timestamped, attributable. The thing a post-incident report is folded from, not a free-text box at the end |
| M1-05 | **Closure with evidence** — outcome, notes, attachments | Evidence is referenced, never inlined into the event log. Retention is **R-11**, unanswered, so nothing is deleted yet and that is recorded rather than assumed |
| M1-06 | **The post-incident report** — folded from the log, not typed | If an operator has to retype what the system already knows, the system has failed. Exportable, because Q-02 made export the point rather than integration |
| M1-07 | **The M1 gate, my half** | Full lifecycle end to end in a browser, and the 15-second rapid-intake budget re-measured on a throttled handset. The operator half is **R-12** |

### The estimate

M1 was the risk I named and it was the right one to name — it was the largest block by some
way. What I did not predict is how much of the week’s value came from **tests finding real
defects rather than from writing features**. Nine, and every one silent in production:

- an emergency reported from a handset was **never routed** — auto-routing lived only in the
  `POST /incidents` path, and the field path goes through `/sync`
- a backdated action **moved the incident's start time**, and every SLA deadline with it
- `runNotifyPass` counted "this department has no post" and wrote nothing to the incident —
  INV-03 violated by a counter in a return value
- `targetsFor` merged an SLA override instead of replacing it, so a department given *more*
  time silently kept the district's deadline
- `setSlaTarget` could create a deadline and never change one
- a phone number leaked into `config_event`, which is rendered on a screen and copied into
  every backup that leaves the district
- the console painted whichever request returned last
- `nextSeatUp` could not see the administration, so escalation out of a department had
  nowhere to go
- the server would not start, caught by `deployable.e2e.test.ts` for the second time

**Nothing slipped, and the reason is that none of those were found by looking for them.**
They were found by walking journeys end to end — which is an argument for the M1 gate
existing, not a coincidence.

## What I will not do this week, and why

- **M2 (three more departments).** Its gate is already met by ADR-0010 — adding a department
  is configuration — but adding them for real needs their routing signals and their rosters,
  which is **R-02** and **R-04**. Adding three empty departments would look like progress and
  produce three more unassigned queues.
- **The Place gazetteer.** Dropped by the owner (Q-08, P-06). Location capture works without
  it.
- **Provincial escalation.** Out of scope (Q-10, ADR-0010). There is no rung above the two
  offices.
- **Public reporting.** Explicitly outside MVP until the district decides (Q-13).

## How this could slip

Stated now rather than explained afterwards.

- **M1 is the largest single block in the project so far**, and the last time I estimated a
  department workspace it turned into six modules. Three days is a target.
- **The integrity sweep (W-01) may find something structural.** 81 posts were loaded before
  routing and rosters existed. If a real inconsistency turns up in the district's own data,
  reporting it honestly matters more than staying on schedule.
- **Anything the tests find gets fixed before I move on.** Four real defects surfaced this way
  in the last two days — a silenced INV-03, an SLA update that never worked, a phone number
  leaking into a log, a render race. That rate is not obviously going to fall.
